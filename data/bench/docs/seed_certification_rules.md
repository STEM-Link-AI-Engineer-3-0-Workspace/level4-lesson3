# Seed Paddy Certification — What the Tags Mean

Teaching corpus. These rules are illustrative and written for a training exercise.

## 1. Classes of seed

Seed paddy passes through four classes on its way from the breeder to the farmer: breeder seed, foundation seed, registered seed and certified seed. Each class is grown from the class before it, under inspection, so that the variety stays true to type.

Farmers normally buy certified seed. Registered seed is sold mainly to seed farmers who will multiply it.

## 2. The tag colours

Every bag of inspected seed carries a tag sewn into the mouth of the bag:

- A white tag means foundation seed.
- A purple tag means registered seed.
- A blue tag means certified seed.

A bag with no tag, or with a tag stapled or tied instead of sewn, has not passed inspection and should not be bought as certified seed.

## 3. What the tag certifies

The tag gives the variety, the lot number, the date of testing and the germination percentage. Certified seed must have a germination of at least 80 percent, physical purity of at least 98 percent, and not more than 10 seeds of other varieties per kilogram. Seed is valid for six months from the date of testing printed on the tag.

## 4. Seed rate

The recommended seed rate for broadcast sowing is 100 kg of certified seed per hectare. Using farmer-saved seed of low germination is the main reason farmers sow far above this rate, which raises cost and plant density without raising yield.

## 5. Complaints

A farmer who finds that certified seed germinated poorly or produced a mixed crop may complain to the seed certification office with the tag and the bag. Keep the tag until the crop is harvested; without it, no complaint can be investigated.
