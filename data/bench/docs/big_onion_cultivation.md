# Big Onion Cultivation Guide

Teaching corpus. Figures are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. Season and seed

Big onion is grown mainly in the Yala season in the dry and intermediate zones, as an irrigated crop after paddy or on well-drained upland. Seed is sown in nursery beds in March and seedlings are transplanted after 6 to 7 weeks. Use fresh seed; onion seed loses germination quickly and seed older than one year often fails.

## 2. Land preparation and spacing

Prepare raised beds 1 m wide and 15 cm high so that water does not stand around the bulbs. Transplant at 15 cm between rows and 10 cm between plants. Big onion has shallow roots and does not compete well with weeds, so beds must be kept clean for the first six weeks.

## 3. Fertilizer for big onion

Apply 10 t/ha of compost at land preparation. As basal fertilizer, apply 50 kg/ha urea, 175 kg/ha TSP and 80 kg/ha MOP. Top dress with 50 kg/ha urea at 3 weeks and 50 kg/ha urea with 40 kg/ha MOP at 6 weeks after transplanting. Stop nitrogen after 8 weeks; late nitrogen produces thick necks and bulbs that rot in storage.

## 4. Onion thrips

Onion thrips are tiny insects that hide in the leaf folds and rasp the leaf surface. Affected leaves show silvery white streaks that later turn brown from the tip, and heavily attacked plants produce small bulbs. Thrips multiply fastest in hot, dry weather. Overhead sprinkler irrigation washes many of them off the leaves. Spray only when more than 30 thrips are found per plant.

## 5. Purple blotch

Purple blotch is a fungal disease that produces small water-soaked spots that enlarge into purple lesions with yellow margins. Leaves bend over and dry from the lesion upward. It spreads in humid weather and after rain. Spray mancozeb 80 WP at 2 g per litre at 10-day intervals once the first lesions appear.

## 6. Harvest and curing

Stop irrigation two weeks before harvest. Harvest when about 70 percent of the tops have fallen over. Cure the bulbs in the shade for 7 to 10 days with the tops on, then cut the tops leaving 2 cm of neck. Bulbs that are not cured properly lose weight and rot in storage.
