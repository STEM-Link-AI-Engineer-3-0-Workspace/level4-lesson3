# Post-Harvest Handling of Paddy

Teaching corpus. Figures are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. When to harvest

Harvest when about 80 to 85 percent of the grains in the panicle are straw-coloured, usually 28 to 32 days after flowering. Harvesting too early gives immature, chalky grains; harvesting too late causes grain shattering and cracking.

## 2. Threshing

Thresh within 24 hours of cutting. Paddy left in heaps in the field heats up, discolours and loses quality. Combine harvesters cut and thresh in one operation but cause higher losses on lodged crops.

## 3. Drying

Freshly harvested paddy contains 20 to 25 percent moisture. Dry it to 14 percent moisture for sale and to 13 percent for storage longer than three months. Dry on tarpaulins or a concrete floor, in layers no thicker than 5 cm, and turn the grain every 30 minutes. Do not dry paddy on the road; it picks up stones and dirt, and the price falls.

## 4. Testing moisture without a meter

Bite a few grains. Grains that break with a clean cracking sound are dry enough for sale. Grains that bend or are soft need more drying.

## 5. Storage

Store dry paddy in clean bags on wooden pallets, at least 50 cm away from walls, in a dry, ventilated store. Keep different varieties separate and label each stack with the variety and date. Inspect monthly for insects and for warm patches, which show that moisture is too high.

## 6. Storage pests

The rice weevil and the lesser grain borer attack stored paddy. Sun-drying the grain again kills most adult insects. Clean the store thoroughly between seasons and remove old grain residues where the insects survive.
