# Paddy Fertilizer Recommendations — Field Guide

Teaching corpus. Figures are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. Why the schedule depends on variety age

Paddy varieties in Sri Lanka are grouped by how long they take from sowing to harvest. The common age classes are 3 months, 3½ months and 4 months. A longer-duration variety stays in the field longer, so it needs its nitrogen spread over more applications. Applying the whole nitrogen dose early to a 4 month variety feeds weeds and encourages lodging later in the season.

Always confirm the age class of the seed you bought before planning fertilizer. The age class is printed on the certified seed bag and is listed in the variety guide.

## 2. Basal dressing

Basal fertilizer is applied at the last ploughing, before sowing or transplanting, and incorporated into the mud. For all age classes under irrigated conditions the basal dressing is 62 kg/ha of TSP (triple super phosphate) and 30 kg/ha of MOP (muriate of potash). No urea is applied as basal for broadcast-sown fields, because seedlings cannot use it in the first two weeks and most of it is lost.

Where a soil test shows available phosphorus above 20 ppm, the TSP basal dose may be halved. Where the soil is sandy and potassium is known to leach, split the MOP into two equal parts and give the second part with the second top dressing.

## 3. Top dressing schedule for 3 month varieties

Short-duration varieties receive urea in three top dressings:

- First top dressing at 2 weeks after sowing: 50 kg/ha urea.
- Second top dressing at 4 weeks after sowing: 75 kg/ha urea.
- Third top dressing at 6 weeks after sowing: 50 kg/ha urea, applied only if the crop shows pale leaves at the panicle initiation stage.

The total urea for a 3 month variety is therefore 175 kg/ha when all three applications are given.

## 4. Top dressing schedule for 3½ month varieties

Medium-duration varieties receive urea in four top dressings at 2, 4, 6 and 7 weeks after sowing, at 50, 75, 50 and 25 kg/ha respectively. The fourth application is a light dose meant to support grain filling and should be skipped if the crop has already lodged in patches.

## 5. Top dressing schedule for 4 month varieties

Long-duration varieties such as Bg 403 and Bg 94-1 receive urea in four top dressings at 2, 5, 7 and 9 weeks after sowing. The rates are 50, 75, 75 and 25 kg/ha. Because the crop stays in the field through most of the Maha rains, nitrogen losses through runoff are higher and the third application is the most important one. Do not apply the last top dressing after the flag leaf has fully emerged.

## 6. Yala and Maha adjustments

In the Yala season, rainfall is lower and solar radiation is higher, so the crop can use nitrogen more efficiently. Reduce every urea top dressing by 10 percent in Yala under fully irrigated conditions. In the Maha season, keep the full rates but delay any application by a few days if heavy rain is forecast within 48 hours, since urea broadcast onto flooded fields before a storm is largely washed away.

## 7. Zinc deficiency

Zinc deficiency appears two to four weeks after transplanting as stunted plants with rusty brown spots on older leaves and a bronzed look across the field. It is common on poorly drained soils and on soils with high pH. Correct it with zinc sulphate at 10 kg/ha mixed with sand and broadcast, or as a 0.5 percent foliar spray. Zinc deficiency is not corrected by adding more urea; extra nitrogen makes the symptoms look worse.

## 8. Organic matter

Where cattle manure or compost is available, apply 5 t/ha at land preparation and reduce the MOP basal dose by half. Rice straw from the previous season can be incorporated instead of burnt; it returns most of the potassium the crop removed.
