# Water Management in Irrigated Paddy

Teaching corpus. Figures are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. How much water a paddy field needs

A paddy crop uses between 1,200 and 1,500 mm of water over a season, including land preparation. Land preparation alone takes 150 to 250 mm. Most of the remaining water is lost to seepage and percolation, not used by the plants. Keeping fields permanently flooded is a habit, not a requirement of the crop.

## 2. Alternate wetting and drying

Alternate wetting and drying, known as AWD, is a method in which the field is allowed to dry out between irrigations instead of being kept flooded. A field water tube, a 30 cm length of perforated plastic pipe pushed into the soil, shows the water level below the surface.

Irrigate when the water level in the tube has fallen 15 cm below the soil surface, and flood the field to about 5 cm. Keep a continuous 5 cm of water from one week before to one week after flowering, because the crop is sensitive to water stress at that stage. AWD saves 15 to 30 percent of irrigation water with no loss of yield when practised correctly.

## 3. Drainage before harvest

Drain the field 10 to 14 days before harvest. Draining earlier reduces grain filling; draining later makes harvesting difficult and delays the next land preparation.

## 4. Minor tanks and rotational issues

In minor tank command areas, water is issued by rotation, and each tract receives water on fixed days. Farmers at the tail end of the canal often receive water last and in smaller amounts. The farmer organisation sets the rotation at the kanna meeting held before each season. Variety choice should match the issue period agreed at that meeting; planting a 4 month variety where water will be issued for only 3 months is the most common cause of crop failure in minor tank areas.

## 5. When the water runs out

If the tank is expected to run dry before the crop matures, prioritise the flowering stage. A crop that suffers water stress during tillering recovers partly; a crop that suffers it during flowering produces empty grains that do not recover. Where only part of the area can be saved, concentrate water on fewer fields rather than spreading too little over all of them.

## 6. Salinity and coastal fields

In coastal fields, seawater can intrude through drainage canals in the dry season. Keep sluice gates closed at high tide, flush fields with fresh water before planting, and choose a salinity-tolerant variety. The variety guide lists which varieties tolerate salinity.
