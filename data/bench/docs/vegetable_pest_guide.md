# Pests of Vegetables — Tomato, Brinjal and Okra

Teaching corpus. Figures and products are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. Whitefly on tomato

Whiteflies are small white insects that fly up in a cloud when the plant is shaken. They suck sap from the underside of leaves and spread tomato yellow leaf curl virus. Infected plants have small, upward-curled leaves with yellow edges and set few fruits. Use virus-free seedlings raised under insect-proof net, remove infected plants early, and use yellow sticky traps to monitor the population.

## 2. Spider mites on brinjal

Spider mites live in colonies on the underside of brinjal leaves, often under fine webbing. Leaves develop tiny pale specks, then turn yellow and dry. Mites increase rapidly in hot, dry weather and after broad-spectrum insecticides kill their predators. Spray wettable sulphur to the underside of the leaves, and avoid repeated pyrethroid use.

## 3. Brinjal shoot and fruit borer

The caterpillar of this moth bores into tender shoots, which wilt and droop, and later into the fruits, leaving holes plugged with excreta. It is the most damaging pest of brinjal. Cut and destroy wilted shoots every week. Remove and destroy bored fruits rather than leaving them in the field. Pheromone traps at 100 per hectare reduce the moth population.

## 4. Okra yellow vein mosaic

Okra yellow vein mosaic is a virus spread by whitefly. The veins of infected leaves turn bright yellow while the tissue between them stays green, giving a net-like pattern. Fruits from infected plants are pale and fibrous. Grow a resistant variety, remove infected plants and control weeds that host whitefly.

## 5. Fruit flies on bitter gourd and cucumber

Fruit fly females lay eggs under the skin of young fruits. Maggots feed inside and the fruits rot and drop. Bag young fruits with paper bags, collect and bury fallen fruits, and use protein bait or cue-lure traps.

## 6. General rule

Most outbreaks of sucking pests on vegetables follow the use of broad-spectrum insecticides early in the season. Natural enemies such as ladybird beetles, lacewings and predatory mites keep sucking pests below damaging levels when they are not killed by sprays.
