# Chilli Cultivation Guide

Teaching corpus. Figures are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. Nursery

Raise chilli seedlings in raised nursery beds 1 m wide, under a shade net or a thin coconut-leaf cover. Sow seeds in lines 10 cm apart and cover lightly with sieved compost. Cover the nursery with insect-proof net from the day of sowing; most leaf curl infections begin in the nursery, carried by insects that feed on young seedlings.

Seedlings are ready to transplant 5 to 6 weeks after sowing, when they have 5 to 6 true leaves.

## 2. Transplanting and spacing

Transplant in the evening onto ridges, at a spacing of 60 cm between rows and 45 cm between plants. Water immediately after planting. Fill gaps within one week.

## 3. Fertilizer for chilli

Apply 10 t/ha of compost or well-rotted manure at land preparation. As basal fertilizer, apply 65 kg/ha urea, 270 kg/ha TSP and 50 kg/ha MOP. Top dress with 65 kg/ha urea and 50 kg/ha MOP at 4 weeks and again at 8 weeks after transplanting. Do not let fertilizer touch the stem.

## 4. Leaf curl complex

Chilli leaf curl is the most damaging problem of the crop. Leaves curl upward or downward, become small, thick and crinkled, and the plant stops producing flowers. The complex is caused by a combination of thrips, mites and a virus spread by whitefly.

- Thrips cause upward curling and silvery scars on the underside of leaves.
- Mites cause downward curling, with leaves that look leathery and bronzed underneath.
- Whitefly spreads a virus that causes yellow, puckered leaves and severe stunting. Infected plants do not recover.

Remove and destroy plants with virus symptoms as soon as they appear. Use yellow sticky traps at 20 per hectare to monitor whitefly. For mites, spray sulphur 80 WG at 3 g per litre to the underside of the leaves. Do not spray the same insecticide more than twice a season.

## 5. Anthracnose of fruit

Anthracnose produces sunken, dark, circular spots on ripening pods, often with rings of pink spore masses. It spreads in wet weather and from infected seed. Use seed from healthy pods only, pick ripe pods promptly, and spray mancozeb 80 WP at 2 g per litre during prolonged wet spells.

## 6. Harvesting

Green chilli is picked from about 75 days after transplanting, every 7 to 10 days. For dry chilli, harvest fully red pods and dry them on raised mats; drying on bare ground causes fungal contamination and lowers the price.
