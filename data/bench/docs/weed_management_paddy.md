# Weed Management in Paddy

Teaching corpus. Figures and products are illustrative and are not official recommendations.

## 1. Why the first month matters

Weeds that emerge with the crop compete for nitrogen, light and water. Most yield loss from weeds happens in the first 30 to 40 days after sowing. A field kept weed-free for this period seldom loses yield to later weeds.

## 2. Main weeds

The main weeds of broadcast paddy are grasses such as barnyard grass and weedy rice, sedges, and broad-leaved weeds. Weedy rice looks like cultivated paddy in its early stages but grows taller, has a reddish tinge at the base and sheds its grains before harvest, so it builds up in the soil from season to season.

## 3. Land preparation and water

Good levelling and a thin layer of standing water after crop establishment suppress most grass weeds. High spots in a badly levelled field are where weeds establish first. Use certified seed, since seed contaminated with weedy rice is the main way it enters a clean field.

## 4. Pre-emergence herbicides

A pre-emergence herbicide is applied before weeds emerge, usually within 3 to 5 days after sowing, onto moist soil. Keep the field saturated but not flooded for a few days after application. Do not apply it to fields where the seed has not yet germinated in standing water.

## 5. Post-emergence herbicides

Post-emergence herbicides are applied when weeds are at the 2 to 4 leaf stage, around 12 to 18 days after sowing. Propanil controls barnyard grass but must not be applied within 10 days of a carbamate or organophosphate insecticide, because the combination damages the crop. Read the label for the correct rate and for the interval before re-flooding.

## 6. Hand weeding

Hand weeding at 3 and 6 weeks after sowing is effective in transplanted fields where rows make walking easy. In broadcast fields, a single hand weeding of weedy rice before it flowers prevents most seed shedding.
