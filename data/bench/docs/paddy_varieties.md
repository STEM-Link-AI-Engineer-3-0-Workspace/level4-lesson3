# Paddy Variety Guide

Teaching corpus. Variety attributes are illustrative and are not official descriptions.

Varieties are named by the research station that bred them and a number. Bg varieties come from Bathalegoda, At from Ambalantota, Ld from Labuduwa and Bw from Bombuwela. The number gives no information about quality or age; check the table entries below.

## 3 month varieties

**Bg 250.** Age 2½ to 3 months. White, short round grain. Suitable for areas where water is available for only a short period. Moderately susceptible to blast.

**Bg 300.** Age 3 months. White, long grain. Widely grown in Yala in the dry zone. Tolerant to brown planthopper. Yield potential about 7 t/ha.

**At 307.** Age 3 months. Red, short round grain. Suitable for both seasons in the intermediate zone. Tolerant to iron toxicity, which makes it a common choice for poorly drained low-country fields.

**Bg 310.** Age 3 months. Red, short round grain, good cooking quality. Susceptible to bacterial leaf blight; avoid it in fields with a history of the disease.

## 3½ month varieties

**Bg 352.** Age 3½ months. White, short round grain. One of the most widely grown varieties in the dry zone. Moderately resistant to blast.

**Bg 357.** Age 3½ months. White, samba-type grain. Popular for its grain quality, but lodges under heavy nitrogen.

**Bg 360.** Age 3½ months. White, keeri samba grain. Fetches a premium price. Susceptible to brown planthopper, so monitor closely from the fourth week.

**At 362.** Age 3½ months. Red, long grain. Resistant to bacterial leaf blight and recommended for fields where the disease recurs.

**Bg 366.** Age 3½ months. White, long grain. Resistant to blast and moderately resistant to brown planthopper.

## 4 month varieties

**Bg 403.** Age 4 months. Red, short round grain. Tall, with good tolerance to deep water; suited to Maha in areas that flood briefly. Susceptible to neck blast.

**Bg 94-1.** Age 4 months. White, long grain. An older variety still grown for its stable yield. Lodges easily on fertile soils.

**Ld 365.** Age 4 months. Red, short round grain. Tolerant to salinity; recommended for coastal fields where seawater intrudes.

**Bw 367.** Age 4 months. Red, medium grain. Tolerant to iron toxicity and to the acid soils of the wet zone.

## Choosing a variety

Choose the age class first, from the length of the water supply you can rely on. Then choose among varieties of that age for the pest and soil problems your field actually has. A premium grain type is worth little if the variety is susceptible to the pest that recurs on your land.
